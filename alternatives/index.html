7 Best OpenRouter Alternatives in 2026: Compared by Fee, Models and Use Case

Short answer: The best OpenRouter alternative in 2026 depends on your priority: LiteLLM for self-hosting and full control, Portkey for governance, TENSORAXIS for transparent per-token rates and domestic models behind one key, Cloudflare or Vercel AI Gateway if you already use those stacks, and Together AI for open-weight inference.

Editorial comparison, not a load test. Provider figures were checked against official pages and published comparisons on 2026-11-18 (see Sources). Confirm current rates before committing.

Disclosure: TENSORAXIS is the product we operate. This is an editorial comparison based on public sources, not an independent benchmark or a third-party user review. Competitor figures are attributed and dated; verify all rates on vendor sites before deciding.

This guide to OpenRouter alternatives compares seven gateways on the criteria that actually matter in 2026: fees, model coverage and deployment model.

OpenRouter made one thing easy: one API key, one OpenAI-compatible endpoint, hundreds of models. For prototyping and mid-volume teams that is still a good deal. Teams start comparing alternatives for structural reasons, not because the product is bad.

Why look for an OpenRouter alternative at all?

  • Credit purchase fee. OpenRouter charges 5.5% on standard card credit purchases with a $0.80 minimum, and 8% on the Business plan. The fee applies to the top-up rather than each token, but the minimum makes small top-ups disproportionately expensive — a $5 purchase carries an effective 16% fee.
  • BYOK is not unlimited. Bring-your-own-key traffic is free up to $25,000/month of list-price inference, then 5%.
  • Hosted only. There is no self-hosted OpenRouter. Traffic and keys pass through its infrastructure, so VPC and data-residency rules push teams toward a gateway they can run themselves.
  • Routing variance. The same model can be routed across backend providers; throughput and quantization may vary by route, which matters in latency-sensitive agent loops.
  • Free-tier limits. Free models are capped at 50 requests/day (1,000/day after $10 in credits) and are not intended for production, per OpenRouter's own FAQ.

1. TENSORAXIS — why choose transparent rates and a domestic lane?

TENSORAXIS (tensoraxis.ai) is a unified AI API gateway: one OpenAI-compatible key covering 50+ upstream providers and 100+ billed models, with 50+ API routes and 10+ scheduling controls. Unlike a pure pass-through router, it publishes per-1M-token input and output rates on its Model Plaza — for example claude-opus-4-8 at $0.42 in / $2.13 out, gemini-3.5-flash at $0.19 / $1.11 and gpt-5.6-terra at $1.5 / $2, with domestic models such as glm-5.3 priced in CNY (¥2.71 / ¥9.49). All figures verified against the official model page on 2026-11-18.

The distinctive feature is the domestic lane: models marked "domestic" are served on China-compliant compute nodes, so teams with regional data requirements can keep those calls in-region while using the same endpoint for frontier models. The site also lists video models — Seedance 2.0 (1080P, 30-second clips, about 35 seconds to generate) and HappyHorse 1.1 (1080P, 15s, about 42s) — which text-first routers generally do not expose.

Trade-off: the catalog is smaller than OpenRouter's 680+ models, and the gateway is managed, though private deployment is available to enterprise customers.

Best fit: teams that want a readable rate card, Chinese models behind one key, and media generation alongside chat.

2. LiteLLM — is the self-hosted default right for you?

LiteLLM is a Python proxy that normalizes 100+ providers behind an OpenAI-compatible API. The open-source core is free under MIT and you bring your own keys: no platform fee, no middleman margin. Enterprise adds RBAC, SSO and audit logging. The cost is operational — you run a PostgreSQL instance, manage deployments and triage upstream changes. For teams without platform engineering capacity, the saved percentage can be smaller than the engineering hours spent maintaining the routing layer.

3. Portkey — do you need governance and observability?

Portkey is a managed AI gateway focused on governance: guardrails, PII redaction, prompt management and observability. It offers an open-source gateway for self-hosting; the full control plane is $49/month on the Production tier with 100,000 logged requests included. Portkey was acquired by Palo Alto Networks in 2026, which signals a direction toward security-led enterprise AI.

4. Cloudflare AI Gateway — best for Cloudflare users?

Cloudflare AI Gateway runs on Cloudflare's edge network. Core gateway features are free; provider inference is passed through at cost, while Cloudflare's Unified Billing applies a 5% fee on credit purchases. It is strongest as a caching and logging layer for applications already on Cloudflare, rather than as a smart cross-provider router.

5. Vercel AI Gateway — native to the Vercel / AI SDK stack?

Vercel AI Gateway is a managed gateway integrated with the Vercel platform and AI SDK. It adds no token markup and gives $5/month in free credits. Convenience is high if you deploy on Vercel; outside that ecosystem, the consolidation benefit largely disappears.

6. Together AI — need open-weight inference at scale?

Together AI is an inference provider, not a router. It offers serverless pay-per-token from roughly $0.03 per million tokens on small models up to $9+ on large reasoning models, dedicated GPU endpoints (an H100 around $5.49–6.49/hour on-demand) and reserved clusters. A Batch API runs at roughly 50% off serverless rates. The real budget risks are fine-tune hosting, dedicated endpoints and retries rather than the headline token rate.

7. Direct provider APIs — want day-one model access?

Going straight to OpenAI, Anthropic or Google gets you new models the day they ship and each provider's batch discounts, at the cost of multiple keys, bills and SDKs. For a single-model workload this is usually the cheapest option — a router only pays off when you need switching, fallback or unified billing.

Is migration just a configuration change?

Any OpenAI-compatible gateway is adopted by changing base_url and api_key in an existing client:

from openai import OpenAI

client = OpenAI(
    base_url="https://api.tensoraxis.ai/v1",
    api_key="YOUR_TENSORAXIS_KEY",
)
response = client.chat.completions.create(
    model="claude-opus-4-8",
    messages=[{"role": "user", "content": "Hello"}],
)

The same swap applies when moving away from OpenRouter. Test chat completions, streaming, tool calling and structured output with your specific models before cutover — advanced features can differ even when the basic endpoint passes.

FAQ

Is there a free OpenRouter alternative? Yes. LiteLLM and Bifrost are open source and free to self-host. Cloudflare AI Gateway is free on all plans, and Vercel AI Gateway includes $5/month in credits. Free tiers trade features for cost.

Which gateway is cheapest? It depends on workload. For a single open model, a direct inference endpoint is often cheapest. For multi-provider routing, compare the fee shape: a percentage favours low spend, while a flat $49/month control plane favours high spend, with a crossover near $900/month of inference. Published per-token cards, such as TENSORAXIS's Model Plaza, make unit costs easier to forecast.

Does OpenRouter mark up token prices? No. It passes through provider token rates and charges the fee at credit purchase. Some other gateways add a percentage to token spend instead.

Can I self-host OpenRouter? No. If in-VPC deployment is required, look at LiteLLM, Bifrost or Portkey's open-source gateway.

Do I need to rewrite code to switch? Usually not. With an OpenAI-compatible endpoint, migration is a base_url and key change. Verify provider-specific parameters and streaming behaviour with your own models.

What about image and video generation? OpenRouter is LLM-first. If your product generates media, check whether the gateway exposes video and image models directly — TENSORAXIS, for example, lists Seedance 2.0 and HappyHorse 1.1 alongside chat models.
