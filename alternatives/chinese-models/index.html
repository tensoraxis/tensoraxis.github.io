OpenRouter Alternative for DeepSeek, Qwen, GLM and Kimi Models

How to access Chinese frontier models without juggling keys, and what to check on data residency. Figures verified 2026-11-18.

Disclosure: TENSORAXIS is the product we operate. This is an editorial comparison based on public sources, not an independent benchmark or a third-party user review. Competitor figures are attributed and dated; verify all rates on vendor sites before deciding.

This is a practical guide to OpenRouter alternatives for teams whose workload centers on Chinese frontier models and data residency.

A global router will happily sell you a Chinese model. The harder questions are what the token rate is, which node serves the request, and whether the data stays where your policy says it should.

Why developers add a Chinese-model lane

DeepSeek, Qwen, GLM and Kimi are frequently cheaper than frontier Western models on comparable reasoning tasks, and some are strong on Chinese-language workloads. OpenRouter lists them among its 680+ models, so access is not the problem. The problems show up at billing and compliance time: the route you get may not be the cheapest endpoint, and a global gateway does not by itself tell you where inference happened.

What should you evaluate in OpenRouter alternatives for Chinese models?

Criterion What to ask
Published rate Is the per-1M input/output price visible before the call?
Node location Can domestic models be pinned to in-region compute?
SDK compatibility Does switching require a base_url change only?
Context window Is the advertised context actually available on the route?
Billing currency Are CNY-priced models billed in CNY or converted with a spread?

Calling a Chinese model with the OpenAI SDK

from openai import OpenAI

client = OpenAI(
    base_url="https://api.tensoraxis.ai/v1",
    api_key="YOUR_TENSORAXIS_KEY",
)
response = client.chat.completions.create(
    model="glm-5.3",
    messages=[{"role": "user", "content": "用一句话解释什么是算力聚合"}],
)
print(response.choices[0].message.content)

Switching model means changing the model field. That is the practical value of an OpenAI-compatible gateway: the same integration covers frontier and domestic models.

Honest trade-offs

  • A text-first global router may still have a broader catalog and automatic fallback across more providers.
  • Pinning a domestic model to an in-region node is useful for compliance but may not be the lowest-latency route for users outside the region.
  • Model IDs and rate cards move quickly; treat any article, including this one, as a snapshot.

FAQ

Can I use one key for both DeepSeek and GPT models? Yes with an aggregator. TENSORAXIS exposes both through a single OpenAI-compatible endpoint; changing the model field switches between them.

Are Chinese models cheaper? Often, but compare the exact route and per-token rate. Published cards make this checkable; a pass-through router may route to a costlier endpoint unless you select otherwise.

How do I know where inference ran? Ask the provider. TENSORAXIS marks domestic models as served on China-compliant compute nodes. For regulated workloads, get the data-residency terms in writing.

Do I need a separate SDK? No. The OpenAI SDK works with the documented base_url.
